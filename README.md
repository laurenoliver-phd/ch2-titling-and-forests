# Summary

Efforts to clarify land ownership are often promoted as a way to improve rural livelihoods while protecting forests. However, it remains unclear whether these interventions deliver lasting benefits for either people or the environment, particularly in light of climate and population pressures. My study, therefore, examines the long-term effects of land tenure interventions on forest use among forest-dependent populations navigating these challenges.

The analysis focuses on a large-scale land demarcation program in Benin, where entire villages were randomly selected for parcel boundary demarcation using GPS and physical markers. In doing so, the intervention aimed to lower the cost of formal land titling, yet only a small share of households ultimately pursued titles. This setting enables an assessment of how both demarcation and subsequent titling shape land use decisions over time in a region where livelihoods are closely tied to natural resources.

I employ a novel administrative dataset containing parcel coordinates and household characteristics for all parcels demarcated under the intervention. This is combined with high-resolution, annual satellite data covering the approximately 30,000 demarcated  parcels located within Benin’s moist tropical forest region. Using these data, I measure deforestation, forest loss, and regrowth within and surrounding demarcated parcels to capture spatial spillovers. I track changes over time and implement an instrumental variables strategy that leverages the randomized rollout of village-level demarcation. My study aims to identify whether demarcation and formal titling generate distinct behavioral responses, heterogeneous effects across households, and evidence of spillovers across neighboring parcels.

The contribution of this research lies in combining large-scale administrative data with fine-resolution satellite imagery to provide causal evidence on long-term outcomes of land tenure interventions. Importantly, it will also address variation in title uptake despite reduced costs and external encouragement, as well as any correlation between behavioral responses and subsequent forest use. These findings will clarify ambiguities surrounding private tenure security and forest use and provide practical insights for policymakers designing interventions to jointly support livelihoods and conservation.

More broadly, the results will shed light on the role of demarcation and titling within forest governance, particularly in regions experiencing environmental and socio-economic change.
